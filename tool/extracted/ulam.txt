1. Sinigang na Baboy
Calories: approximately 600 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Soup/Stew   Difficulty: Medium   Servings: 1 person
A beloved Filipino sour soup made with pork ribs in a tamarind broth with fresh vegetables.
Ingredients
• 150g pork ribs, cut into serving pieces
• 1/2 medium tomato, quartered
• 1/4 medium onion, quartered
• 1/2 pc siling haba
• 1/4 medium radish, sliced
• 1/4 medium eggplant, sliced
• 1/4 bundle kangkong, stems trimmed
• 10g sinigang mix or 30g fresh tamarind
• 500ml water
• 1 tbsp fish sauce, or to taste
Steps
1. In a large pot, bring 2 liters of water to a boil. Add pork ribs and blanch for 5 minutes. Discard water and rinse pork.
2. Return pork to pot with fresh 2 liters water. Add tomatoes and onion. Boil then simmer for 45-60 minutes until pork is fork-tender.
3. Skim scum regularly for a clear broth.
4. Add tamarind mix or strained fresh tamarind juice. Stir and taste.
5. Add radish and eggplant. Simmer 5 minutes.
6. Add siling haba and kangkong. Cook 2 minutes more.
7. Season with fish sauce. Serve hot with steamed rice.
Cooking Tips
For richer broth, use bone-in pork ribs or pork neck bones.
Add 1 tsp of miso paste for umami depth (Miso Sinigang variation).
Vegetables should be added from firmest to most tender to avoid overcooking.
Tamarind mix can be adjusted — add gradually and taste as you go.
2. Adobong Manok
Calories: approximately 650 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Braised   Difficulty: Easy   Servings: 1 person
The quintessential Filipino dish — chicken braised in vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, and bay leaves.
Ingredients
• 150g chicken, preferably bone-in thigh or drumstick
• 2 tbsp vinegar
• 1 tbsp soy sauce
• 3 cloves garlic, crushed
• 1 pc bay leaf
• 1/4 tsp whole black peppercorns
• 1/4 cup water
• 1 tsp cooking oil
Steps
1. Combine chicken, vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, bay leaves, and peppercorns in a pot. Marinate 30 minutes to 1 hour.
2. Add water. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat without stirring.
3. Once boiling, lower heat and simmer for 25-30 minutes until chicken is tender.
4. Remove chicken and pan-fry in hot oil until golden brown.
5. Return to pot and reduce remaining sauce until thick and glossy.
6. Serve with steamed rice.
Cooking Tips
Never stir vinegar when it first hits the heat — let the acidity cook off to avoid bitterness.
The secret to great adobo is the final fry — it adds depth and color.
White cane vinegar is traditional; use coconut vinegar for a milder, more fragrant result.
Adobo keeps for days refrigerated and tastes better the next day.
3. Kare-Kare
Calories: approximately 1050 kcal per serving
Region: Central Luzon/Pampanga   Category: Soup/Stew   Difficulty: Hard   Servings: 1 person
A rich peanut-based stew with oxtail and vegetables, always served with bagoong alamang.
Ingredients
• 150g oxtail
• 100g banana blossom
• 1/4 medium eggplant
• 60g sitaw
• 1 tbsp peanut butter
• 1 tbsp ground toasted peanuts
• 1 tsp annatto powder dissolved in 1/4 cup water
• 1/4 medium onion
• 1 clove garlic
• 1 tsp cooking oil
• 1 tsp fish sauce
• Salt to taste
• 1 tbsp bagoong alamang, for serving
Steps
1. Pressure cook oxtail with enough water for 45 minutes or boil for 2-3 hours until very tender. Reserve broth (at least 4 cups).
2. In a large pot, sauté garlic and onion in oil until soft.
3. Add the annatto water; stir and cook 2 minutes.
4. Add peanut butter and ground peanuts. Stir well.
5. Pour in reserved oxtail broth. Bring to simmer.
6. Add oxtail. Simmer 10 minutes.
7. Add eggplant, banana blossom, and sitaw. Cook until vegetables are tender, about 8-10 minutes.
8. Season with fish sauce and salt. Sauce should be thick and creamy.
9. Serve with a generous side of sautéed bagoong alamang.
Cooking Tips
The key to great kare-kare is the balance of peanut richness without being too heavy — use broth to adjust consistency.
Toast peanuts yourself and grind coarsely for superior flavor over store-bought peanut butter alone.
Blanch banana blossom in salted water with a splash of vinegar to prevent browning.
Always serve bagoong on the side — it provides the salt and umami balance.
4. Lechon Kawali
Calories: approximately 1420 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Grilled/Fried   Difficulty: Medium   Servings: 1 person
Deep-fried pork belly with shatteringly crispy skin and tender meat — the ultimate Filipino pork dish.
Ingredients
• 180g pork belly slab
• 1/2 tsp salt
• 1/4 tsp black pepper
• 1 pc bay leaf
• 2 cloves garlic, crushed
• Water for boiling
• Oil for deep-frying
Steps
1. Place pork belly in a pot. Cover with water. Add salt, pepper, bay leaves, and garlic.
2. Boil then simmer for 45-60 minutes until pork is very tender (fork goes through easily).
3. Remove pork; cool to room temperature.
4. Refrigerate uncovered overnight (or at least 4 hours) — this dries the skin for maximum crispiness.
5. Heat oil to 180°C in a deep pot or kawali.
6. Carefully lower pork into hot oil. Fry 15-20 minutes until deep golden and very crispy.
7. Drain on wire rack (not paper towels — steam makes skin soggy).
8. Rest 5 minutes before chopping. Serve with liver sauce or spiced vinegar.
Cooking Tips
The overnight drying step is non-negotiable for crispy skin.
Score the skin lightly before boiling to help it blister during frying.
Fry at consistent high heat — do not keep adjusting the temperature.
For extra crispiness, rub salt into the skin side before refrigerating.
5. Tinolang Manok
Calories: approximately 520 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Soup/Stew   Difficulty: Easy   Servings: 1 person
Light and nourishing ginger chicken soup with green papaya and malunggay leaves.
Ingredients
• 150g chicken pieces
• 150g green papaya, peeled and sliced
• 1/2 cup malunggay leaves
• 1-inch ginger, julienned
• 1/4 medium onion, sliced
• 1 clove garlic, minced
• 1 tbsp fish sauce
• 500ml water
• 1 tsp cooking oil
Steps
1. Heat oil in pot over medium heat. Sauté ginger until fragrant, about 1 minute.
2. Add garlic and onion; cook until soft.
3. Add chicken pieces. Cook, turning, until lightly browned on all sides.
4. Pour in water. Bring to a boil; skim scum.
5. Add green papaya. Simmer 15-20 minutes until papaya and chicken are tender.
6. Season with fish sauce.
7. Add malunggay leaves. Cook 1 minute more.
8. Serve hot with rice.
Cooking Tips
Use bone-in chicken for a more flavorful broth.
Siling haba (green chili) can be added for a subtle heat.
Malunggay wilts quickly — add it last and don't overcook.
Chayote can substitute green papaya if unavailable.
6. Bistek Tagalog
Calories: approximately 1030 kcal per serving
Region: Tagalog   Category: Braised   Difficulty: Easy   Servings: 1 person
Thinly sliced beef braised in calamansi and soy sauce, topped with caramelized onion rings.
Ingredients
• 150g beef sirloin or round, thinly sliced
• 1 1/2 tbsp calamansi juice
• 1 tbsp soy sauce
• 1/2 medium onion, sliced into rings
• 1 clove garlic, minced
• 1/8 tsp black pepper
• 1 tsp cooking oil
• Salt to taste
Steps
1. Marinate beef in calamansi juice, soy sauce, garlic, and black pepper for at least 30 minutes.
2. Heat oil in pan over high heat. Sear beef slices quickly, about 1 minute per side. Remove and set aside.
3. In same pan, cook onion rings until slightly softened and lightly caramelized.
4. Return beef to pan with marinade. Simmer 2-3 minutes until sauce slightly thickens.
5. Taste and adjust — add more calamansi for sourness, soy for saltiness.
6. Serve over steamed rice, topped with onion rings.
Cooking Tips
Slice beef paper-thin for quick cooking and tenderness — freeze slightly before slicing.
Do not overcook; bistek should be tender, not tough.
The sauce should be slightly tart — calamansi is key and cannot be replaced with lemon alone.
Caramelize the onions properly — this sweetness balances the sour-salty sauce.
7. Pork Dinuguan
Calories: approximately 1850 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Soup/Stew   Difficulty: Medium   Servings: 1 person
Rich, savory, and slightly sour pork stew in fresh pork blood — the Filipino's beloved 'chocolate meat'.
Ingredients
• 150g pork shoulder, cubed
• 75g pork intestine, cleaned and cut
• 1/4 cup fresh pork blood
• 1 1/2 tbsp vinegar
• 1 clove garlic
• 1/4 medium onion
• 1 pc siling haba
• 1 tsp cooking oil
• Fish sauce and salt to taste
Steps
1. Boil pork intestines in water with garlic and vinegar for 30 minutes until tender. Drain and cut.
2. In a pot, heat oil and sauté garlic and onion until fragrant.
3. Add pork shoulder; cook until browned.
4. Add intestines; stir.
5. Pour in pork blood. Stir constantly while bringing to a simmer to prevent lumps.
6. Add vinegar; do not stir for 2 minutes (let it cook off).
7. Add siling haba. Simmer 15-20 minutes until sauce thickens.
8. Season with fish sauce and salt. Serve with puto or steamed rice.
Cooking Tips
Use fresh pork blood from the palengke on the day of cooking.
Stir blood as you add it to prevent it from clumping.
Sourness is key — taste and add vinegar to balance the richness.
Cleaning intestines thoroughly (inside out, with salt and vinegar) removes odor.
8. Paksiw na Isda
Calories: approximately 800 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Braised   Difficulty: Easy   Servings: 1 person
Fish braised in vinegar and ginger — a quick, economical, and delicious Filipino everyday dish.
Ingredients
• 150g bangus or tilapia, sliced
• 2 tbsp white vinegar
• 1/4 cup water
• 1-inch ginger, sliced
• 1/4 medium onion
• 1 clove garlic
• 1/4 tsp whole peppercorns
• 1/2 pc siling haba
• 1 tsp cooking oil
• Salt or fish sauce to taste
Steps
1. Arrange ginger, garlic, and onion at the bottom of a pot.
2. Layer fish on top.
3. Pour vinegar and water over fish. Add peppercorns and siling haba.
4. Do not stir. Bring to a boil over medium heat.
5. Once boiling, lower heat. Simmer 15 minutes until fish is cooked and liquid slightly reduces.
6. Drizzle cooking oil over the top.
7. Season with salt or fish sauce. Serve hot.
Cooking Tips
Do not stir after adding vinegar — it keeps the fish intact.
Add a tablespoon of bagoong for extra depth.
For bangus, ask your palengke vendor to clean, debone, and butterfly it.
Leftovers reheat well and are delicious the next day.
9. Ginisang Ampalaya
Calories: approximately 180 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Sautéed   Difficulty: Easy   Servings: 1 person
Bitter melon sautéed with egg, tomatoes, and shrimp paste — a nutritious Filipino staple.
Ingredients
• 1/2 medium ampalaya, sliced
• 1 large egg, beaten
• 2 cloves garlic, minced
• 1/4 medium onion
• 1/2 medium tomato
• 1 tsp bagoong alamang
• Salt and pepper
• 1 tsp cooking oil
Steps
1. Rub sliced ampalaya with 1 tbsp salt. Let sit 15 minutes. Squeeze firmly to remove bitter liquid. Rinse and drain.
2. Heat oil in pan over medium heat. Sauté garlic until golden.
3. Add onion and tomatoes; cook until soft.
4. Add bagoong; stir and cook 1 minute.
5. Add ampalaya. Stir-fry 3-5 minutes until tender-crisp.
6. Pour beaten eggs over ampalaya; let set briefly then scramble gently.
7. Season with salt and pepper. Serve immediately.
Cooking Tips
Salting and squeezing is essential to reduce bitterness — don't skip.
Do not overcook ampalaya; it should retain a slight bite.
Add shrimp or ground pork for a heartier dish.
Some cooks blanch ampalaya in boiling water before sautéing for less bitterness.
10. Bulalo
Calories: approximately 650 kcal per serving
Region: Batangas   Category: Soup/Stew   Difficulty: Hard   Servings: 1 person
A majestic Batangas beef soup with bone marrow, corn, and vegetables — slow-cooked for hours.
Ingredients
• 200g beef shank with bone marrow
• 1/2 pc corn
• 1/8 small cabbage
• 1/4 bundle pechay
• 1/4 medium onion
• 1/4 tsp whole peppercorns
• 1 tbsp fish sauce
• 1 liter water
Steps
1. Blanch beef shank in boiling water for 5 minutes. Discard water; rinse.
2. Return beef to pot. Cover with fresh 3 liters water. Add onion and peppercorns.
3. Bring to a boil; skim scum thoroughly.
4. Simmer on low heat for 2.5-3 hours until beef is very tender and marrow is soft.
5. Add corn. Cook 20 minutes.
6. Add cabbage and pechay. Cook 5 minutes until tender.
7. Season generously with fish sauce.
8. Serve piping hot with corn, marrow, and all the vegetables.
Cooking Tips
Never rush bulalo — low and slow is the only way.
Skim the scum in the first 30 minutes for a clear, clean broth.
Scoop the marrow from the bone straight into your bowl — that's the best part.
Season only at the end; the long simmer concentrates flavors naturally.
11. Laing
Calories: approximately 3900 kcal per serving
Region: Bicol   Category: Braised   Difficulty: Medium   Servings: 1 person
Dried taro leaves slowly simmered in rich coconut milk with pork and fiery bird's eye chilis.
Ingredients
• 50g dried taro leaves
• 100g pork belly
• 150ml thin coconut milk
• 75ml coconut cream
• 1 tbsp bagoong alamang
• 1 pc siling haba
• 1 pc siling labuyo
• 2 cloves garlic
• 1/4 medium onion
• 1/2-inch ginger
Steps
1. Layer dried taro leaves at the bottom of a wide pot. Do NOT wash them.
2. Scatter garlic, onion, and ginger over the leaves.
3. Place pork on top. Add bagoong and all chilis.
4. Pour thin coconut milk over everything. Do NOT stir.
5. Bring to a boil over medium heat. Do not stir.
6. Lower heat; simmer uncovered 20-25 minutes until coconut milk is mostly absorbed.
7. Gently pour thick coconut cream over the top. Stir once or twice only.
8. Simmer 10-15 more minutes until cream reduces and oil separates.
9. Taste and adjust with more bagoong if needed. Serve with rice.
Cooking Tips
CRITICAL: Use fully dried taro leaves only — never fresh (causes itching).
CRITICAL: Never stir until the thin coconut milk is absorbed.
The dish is done when oil separates on the surface — that's the authentic finish.
Tastes even better the next day as flavors deepen overnight.
12. Bicol Express

Region: Bicol   Category: Braised   Difficulty: Easy   Servings: 1 person
Spicy pork cooked in coconut milk with heaps of chilies and shrimp paste.
Ingredients
• 150g pork belly
• 150ml coconut milk
• 75ml coconut cream
• 1 pc siling haba
• 1 pc siling labuyo
• 1 tbsp bagoong alamang
• 2 cloves garlic
• 1/4 medium onion
• 1 tsp cooking oil
Steps
1. Heat oil; sauté garlic and onion until soft.
2. Add pork belly. Cook until lightly browned.
3. Add bagoong; stir and cook 2 minutes.

8. Serve with lots of steamed rice.
Cooking Tips
Adjust chilis to your heat tolerance — the original is fiery!
Do not substitute coconut milk with cream from the start; use thin then thick for best texture.
Blanching pork before frying removes excess fat and gaminess.
A pinch of sugar balances the heat and salt beautifully.

14. Pritong Bangus
Calories: approximately 500 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Grilled/Fried   Difficulty: Easy   Servings: 1 person
Simple, perfectly fried milkfish — crispy on the outside, flaky and tender inside.
Ingredients
• 1/4 whole bangus, cleaned and butterflied
• 1 clove garlic
• 1 tbsp calamansi juice
• 1/4 tsp salt
• 1/8 tsp black pepper
• 1 tbsp cooking oil
Steps
1. Score the flesh of the bangus with diagonal cuts 1 inch apart.
2. Rub all over with garlic, calamansi juice, salt, and pepper. Marinate 15-30 minutes.
3. Heat generous amount of oil in a large pan over medium-high heat.
4. Fry bangus skin-side up first for 5-7 minutes until golden.
5. Carefully flip; fry skin side 5-7 minutes until very crispy.
6. Drain on wire rack. Serve immediately with tomato-onion salsa or spiced vinegar.
Cooking Tips
Dry the fish well before frying to prevent oil splatter.
Ask your palengke vendor to debone the bangus for you — saves a lot of hassle.
Oil must be hot before adding fish for a crispy result.
Serve with garlic rice and a fried egg for the classic Filipino breakfast plate.
15. Escabeche
Calories: approximately 620 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Sautéed   Difficulty: Medium   Servings: 1 person
Crispy fried fish in a sweet and sour ginger-vegetable sauce.
Ingredients
• 150g fish, cleaned
• 1 tbsp vinegar
• 1 tsp sugar
• 1/4 medium carrot
• 1/4 red bell pepper
• 1/4 green bell pepper
• 1-inch ginger
• 1/4 medium onion
• 1 tsp soy sauce
• 2 tbsp water
• 1 tsp cornstarch dissolved in 1 tbsp water
• Salt and pepper
• 2 tbsp oil for frying
Steps
1. Season fish with salt and pepper. Fry in hot oil until golden crispy. Drain; set on serving plate.
2. In same pan with 2 tbsp oil, sauté ginger and onion.
3. Add carrots and bell peppers; stir-fry 2 minutes.
4. Add vinegar, soy sauce, sugar, and water. Bring to a simmer.
5. Add cornstarch mixture; stir until sauce thickens.
6. Taste — should be sweet, sour, and savory.
7. Pour sauce over fried fish. Serve immediately.
Cooking Tips
Fry the fish very crispy so it holds up under the sauce.
Balance the sweet-sour ratio by tasting the sauce before adding cornstarch.
Add a pinch of salt to the sauce — it enhances all flavors.
Escabeche is also delicious cold the next day.
16. Tokwa't Baboy
Calories: approximately 1050 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Salad/Fresh   Difficulty: Easy   Servings: 1 person
Crispy tofu and boiled pork ears tossed in a tangy vinegar-soy dressing — the perfect pulutan.
Ingredients
• 150g pork ears, cleaned
• 100g firm tokwa
• 1 tbsp vinegar
• 1 tsp soy sauce
• 1 tsp calamansi juice
• 1/2 tsp sugar
• 2 tbsp red onion
• 1/2 pc siling labuyo
• 1 clove garlic
• 1 tbsp oil for frying
Steps
1. Boil pork ears in water with garlic and peppercorns for 1 hour until very tender. Cool; slice thinly.
2. Drain tokwa; press to remove excess moisture. Cube into 1-inch pieces.
3. Fry tokwa in hot oil until golden and crispy on all sides. Drain.
4. Make dressing: combine vinegar, soy sauce, calamansi juice, sugar, onion, chili, and garlic. Mix well.
5. Combine pork ears and tokwa on a plate.
6. Pour dressing over. Toss gently. Serve immediately.
Cooking Tips
Press tofu for at least 30 minutes before frying for maximum crispiness.
Pork ears are best when gelatinous and tender — do not rush the boiling.
The dressing is the star — taste and balance vinegar, soy, and sweetness.
Serve as a side dish, pulutan (drinking snack), or over congee.
17. Caldereta
Calories: approximately 700 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Braised   Difficulty: Hard   Servings: 1 person
Rich, spicy tomato stew with goat or beef, liver spread, and olives — a fiesta staple.
Ingredients
• 150g goat or beef
• 100g tomato sauce
• 40g liver spread
• 1/2 medium potato
• 1/2 medium carrot
• 1/4 red bell pepper
• 2 tbsp green olives
• 1 pc siling labuyo
• 1/4 medium onion
• 1 clove garlic
• 1/4 cup water or broth
• 1 tsp oil
• Salt, pepper, and fish sauce to taste
Steps
1. Marinate meat in vinegar, garlic, and pepper for 1 hour if using goat.
2. Brown meat in hot oil. Remove; set aside.
3. Sauté garlic and onion. Add tomato sauce; cook 3 minutes.
4. Add liver spread; stir until incorporated.
5. Return meat. Add water. Simmer covered 1-1.5 hours until tender.
6. Add potatoes and carrots. Cook 15 minutes.
7. Add bell peppers, olives, and chilis.
8. Season with salt, pepper, fish sauce.
9. Simmer 5 more minutes. Serve with rice.
Cooking Tips
Liver spread is the secret to authentic caldereta richness — don't skip it.
For goat, marinate overnight in vinegar and garlic to remove gaminess.
Cheese (quick-melt) can be added near the end for a creamy variation.
Olives add a sophisticated salty tang — use generously.

20. Chopsuey
Calories: approximately 650 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Sautéed   Difficulty: Easy   Servings: 1 person
Filipino-style stir-fried mixed vegetables with meat and oyster sauce.
Ingredients
• 100g pork or chicken
• 50g shrimp
• 1/8 head cabbage
• 1/2 medium carrot
• 50g snap peas or Baguio beans
• 50g cauliflower
• 2 pcs quail eggs, optional
• 1/4 medium onion
• 1 clove garlic
• 1 tbsp oyster sauce
• 1 tsp soy sauce
• 2 tbsp broth
• 1 tsp cornstarch dissolved in 1 tbsp water
• 1 tsp cooking oil
Steps
1. Blanch hard vegetables (carrots, cauliflower) in salted water for 2 minutes. Drain.
2. Heat oil; sauté garlic and onion.

8. Pour cornstarch mixture to thicken sauce.

Cooking Tips
High heat is key — stir-fry fast to keep vegetables crisp.
Add ingredients in order of cooking time: meat first, hard veg, soft veg last.
Don't overcrowd the pan or vegetables will steam instead of stir-fry.
Baby corn, mushrooms, and young corn cobs are great additions.

22. Inihaw na Liempo
Calories: approximately 1350 kcal per serving
Region: Nationwide   Category: Grilled/Fried   Difficulty: Easy   Servings: 1 person
Sweet-savory grilled pork belly basted with a garlic-ketchup-soy marinade.
Ingredients
• 180g pork belly
• 1 tbsp soy sauce
• 1 tbsp banana ketchup
• 1 tbsp calamansi juice
• 1 clove garlic
• 1 tsp sugar
• 1/4 tsp black pepper
• 1 tbsp 7-Up or Sprite
Steps
1. Score pork belly skin every 2 inches.
2. Combine all marinade ingredients. Marinate pork at least 4 hours or overnight.
3. Prepare charcoal grill. Grill pork belly over medium heat.
4. Baste with marinade every 3-4 minutes.
5. Grill 20-25 minutes, flipping halfway, until cooked through with char marks.
6. Rest 5 minutes before slicing.
7. Serve with spiced vinegar and atchara (pickled papaya).
Cooking Tips
Overnight marinating gives far better flavor penetration.
The 7-Up or soda softens the meat and adds subtle sweetness.
Control the charcoal heat — too hot and it burns before cooking through.
For even cooking, finish over indirect heat after initial searing.
23. Inihaw na Bangus
Calories: approximately 650 kcal per serving
